Babies vs adults

A newborn has roughly 270 to 300 separate bony pieces. An adult has 206. Nothing is lost: bones fuse together as we grow, and the cartilage that makes up much of a baby's skeleton gradually turns to bone. Drag the slider to watch it happen.

    Why so many pieces at birth?

    Many adult bones start life as several separate parts. Each hip bone begins as three, the sacrum as five and the skull as a set of plates. Fusion over childhood and the teens joins them.

    Why cartilage?

    A skeleton that is partly cartilage is flexible enough for birth and can grow quickly. Bone replaces the cartilage in a set sequence, which is why a wrist X-ray can estimate a child's skeletal age.

    Why do counts differ?

    The figure depends on what you count as a bone: ossification centers, sesamoids and small variants all change the tally. Quoted newborn figures range from about 270 to over 300.

    Children break differently

    Children have a thick, active periosteum and open growth plates. Greenstick and buckle (torus) fractures are typical, and growth plate injuries are classified by Salter-Harris type.
